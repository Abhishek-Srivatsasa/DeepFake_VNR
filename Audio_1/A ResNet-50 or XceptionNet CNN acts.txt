A ResNet-50 or XceptionNet CNN acts as a feature extractor to analyze individual frames for facial irregularities (eyes, nose, mouth), while an LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) layer tracks these features over a sequence of 20–30 frames to spot "jerky" transitions or lighting mismatches that AI often fails to synchronize.

MesoNet-4: If your prototype needs to be lightweight (e.g., for a mobile app), MesoNet is a compact CNN specifically designed to detect microscopic facial forgeries in videos.

# Audio 

 Instead of processing raw audio, convert your sound files into Mel Spectrograms (visual representations of sound frequencies). A standard CNN like VGG16 can then treat these spectrograms as images to identify "unnatural" frequency patterns typical of cloned voices.


 For higher accuracy, Wav2Vec 2.0 is currently the state-of-the-art. Unlike basic CNNs, it captures deep semantic and prosodic dependencies in speech, making it highly effective at catching "cloned" voices from platforms like ElevenLabs.

 Use the Librosa library to extract Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCCs). These are the "fingerprints" of a voice; AI-generated speech often lacks the natural pitch variations and vocal-tract resonance found in humans.
